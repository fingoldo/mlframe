# Wave 5: concurrent entry into numba parallel kernels

Read-only audit of `src/mlframe`. Guard: `mlframe/_numba_parallel_guard.py::parallel_kernel_entry` (process-wide RLock, no-op under TBB).
Method: the repo's own call-graph scanner (`mlframe/_nested_parallel_scan.py`) was re-run from a scratch script with two changes:
(a) joblib `Parallel(backend="threading" | prefer="threads")`, `parallel_backend("threading")`, `threading.Thread` and `ThreadPoolExecutor` all count as roots;
(b) every same-named definition is followed (the scanner stops at the first two).
Each hit was then checked by hand. Name-collision hits (`fn`, `run`, `score`) were thrown out.
The scanner sees 295 `parallel=True` kernels.
No crash below has been reproduced. Every High or Medium row is a code-level proof that two threads can enter the same unguarded `parallel=True` kernel at once. Whether that aborts depends on numba's threading layer: it does under `workqueue`, which is what the macOS CI shards fall back to. Confirming the abort needs a run on macOS with `NUMBA_THREADING_LAYER=workqueue`.
