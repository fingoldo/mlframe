NOISE-IN-SELECTION conclusion 2026-11-23 (owner: pairwise redundancy is the Fleuret criterion, a set-wise test is not wanted; PLAN-07 is REJECTED, the out-of-fold boosting test stays a diagnostic only). The Fleuret criterion is the default (mrmr_relevance_algo and mrmr_redundancy_algo both "fleuret", use_simple_mode False) and is applied to every candidate: gain = I(X;Y) - max_k I(X;Y|S_k) against the permutation floor, so a candidate must have significant information given EACH selected feature separately. The pairwise test with equal bins (8 and 16, _benchmarks scripts noise_cmi2) reproduces the gate: opat__x3_x4_x24 given opat__x3_x4_x7 has CMI 0.0082 nat and opat__x3_x4_x7 given it 0.0076, p=0.02 at both bin counts (the opat codes have 6 levels, so the bin count does not change them); x4 (p 0.38), x3__relu (p 0.28), x2 (p 0.16) are the columns the pairwise test calls redundant, as expected. So the gate is not leaking: the two opat features are two different random-reference quantisations of (x3, x4): the pattern of (x3, x4, x24) places x3 and x4 against a random draw, which reveals their magnitudes, and a second draw (x7) reveals more; the information is real and about signal columns, the noise column acts as a dither reference, not as a source of signal. The benchmark column noise-source share therefore counts dither references and SIR inputs as noise; it should be read as an upper bound, and a loading-weighted version is the honest metric (open, low priority). Open question for the owner: whether an ordinal-pattern feature should take its third column only from already selected or strong columns, which would stop the random-reference variants (opat with x24 and x7) from entering at all; they add information per the gate but at a cost of 2 extra columns that a linear model cannot use.
