## Question 1. Does a monotone transform of the target or of a feature change what MRMR selects?

### Setup
MRMR (`verbose=0, n_jobs=1, random_state=0`, defaults otherwise) on n=3000. Fixtures with a known informative set (6 columns, 12 for the graded ones): regression `lognorm` y=exp(0.8x0+0.5x1+0.3x2+0.3e), `cube` y=(3+0.6x0+0.4x1+0.25x2+0.15e)^3, `weak` (x3 weak), `linear`, `inter` y=exp(0.7x0+0.5x1x2+0.3e) (x1, x2 matter only jointly), and `g_lognorm`, `g_linear`, `g_cube` with six informative columns of strength 1, 0.6, 0.35, 0.2, 0.12, 0.08 so some selections sit on the boundary; classification by threshold `c_lin` (cut at the median), `c_skew` (lognormal columns, cut at the 70 % quantile), `c_tail` (cut at the 90 % quantile), `g_clf`. A class label is unchanged by ANY monotone map of the latent score, so for classification only feature maps are meaningful (and the target map is trivially invariant). Variants: target `log y`, `cbrt y`; every feature column through `exp`, `x^3`, `tanh(1.5x)`; plus a refit of the raw data with `random_state=1` (the selection jitter of MRMR itself). `binning_scheme` in {quantile (default), uniform, mdlp} x `fe_max_steps=0`; every variant is compared with the raw fit of the same fixture, scheme and data seed.
Two levels are compared. **final** = the set returned by `get_feature_names_out()`. **MI core** = the rows of `fe_provenance_` that are in the output and have a finite `mrmr_gain` (what the MI greedy chose before later stages) with the largest |gain difference| over the shared rows. Two cuts of the product are shown: the default, and `fe_residual_rescue_enable=False` ("norescue"), because the first run showed the default is NOT invariant and `tt_b5_diag.py` located the cause: with the residual rescue switched off the same case becomes identical (`inter` seed 0, cbrt: default `[x0,x1,x2]` vs `[x0,x1]`; norescue identical), and the rescue is a held-out ridge on the raw `y`, i.e. value-based.

Reading it:
1. **MI core, target map: exactly invariant under all three schemes** (26/26 each, gain differences 0.0 to six digits). Including `uniform`: MRMR does not bin the target equal-width, whatever `binning_scheme` is; the selection is indifferent to `log y` or `cbrt y` in the binning step. So the equal-width-MI "proposer" of PLAN-03 measured a property of that script's private equal-width binning of `f(T)`, not of MRMR.
2. **MI core, feature map: invariant under quantile (52/52) and mdlp (52/52); NOT under uniform (32/52, gain differences up to 0.52 nats).** The only quantile / mdlp deviations are tie cases: `tanh(1.5x)` saturates to exactly 1.0 in float for large lognormal values, making ties, so the map is not strictly monotone after rounding (gain diff 5.7e-3 and 1.5e-2).
3. **Final selected set under the default product is not invariant to a target transform** (quantile 22/32, mdlp 22/32, uniform 21/32), and the non-identical cases are all in `inter` (4/4 differ for every scheme), `weak` and the graded fixtures: the held-out ridge rescue (`fe_residual_rescue_*`, also the ridge in the redundancy 'drop' policy) is the only stage where `f(y)` enters by value. With it off the target is identical in 26/26 (quantile, mdlp) and 25/26 (uniform).
4. The remaining final-set differences are boundary noise of a late stage that appends a weak column with `mrmr_gain` NaN (`support_rank` -1): quantile+norescue feature 48/52 final but 52/52 core; all four differences are the weak column `x3` in graded fixtures, and a plain `random_state` change differs in the same way (jitter 16/17 final, 15/17 core). On fixtures with no boundary feature (`clear`: lognorm, cube, linear, weak, inter, c_*) quantile+norescue is 40/40 (feature) and 20/20 (target) identical.

**Combination table** (fe_max_steps=0): invariant = quantile x {target, feature} at the MI level; mdlp x {target, feature}; uniform x target. Not invariant = uniform x feature (any monotone map of a column changes its equal-width bins), and the default product x target at the level of the final set because of the ridge rescue; borderline columns flip with `random_state` as often as with a map.

### fe_max_steps = 1 (engineered columns)
With `fe_max_steps=1` the candidate set is built from the VALUES of the columns (products, relu hinges, Chebyshev / Hermite bases, random Fourier features, single-index directions), so even a rank-invariant MI gives a different pool when a column is mapped. FE_TABLE_PLACEHOLDER

### Implication for `target_transform_apply` (choose features against f(T))
Under the default `quantile` scheme (and `mdlp`, and in fact `uniform` for the target) the MI of every feature with `f(T)` equals the MI with `T` for any strictly monotone `f`: 26/26 cases with gains identical to six digits. **Selecting features against `f(T)` through MI is pointless: it returns the same features.** The only place where `f(T)` can change the selection is the value-based ridge rescue (and any future linear-usability gate), where the change goes both ways (`inter`: `[x0,x1,x2]` raw vs `[x0]` for `log y`), so it needs its own judge, not the MI. A target transform is worth proposing only for the downstream model (linearity, residual scale), as A1-A5 found; the information criterion that does that is the Jacobian LL of question 2, not MI. Same for features: a monotone map of a column cannot raise its rank-MI, so the FE unary step (`log_abs`, `sqrt`, ...) can only be accepted by a value-based criterion (the Pearson-information gate of A2), never by the MI gain.

## Question 2. Compute cost of the no-retraining criteria

### What was implemented (all one pass over the data in row chunks; no per-candidate Python loop over rows)
**(a) Target criterion** `tt_b5_cost.target_criterion` (K=9 candidates: identity, cbrt, signed sqrt, signed square, log, -1/y, asinh, Yeo-Johnson 0.5 and -0.5; K=10 adds Box-Cox 0.25, verified in the checks). Per half: G=D'D, c=D'f(y) for all K at once, z'z, held-out Jacobian sums; then everything is closed form: `W = (G_A+reg)^-1 c_A`, `MSE_B = (z'z - 2 c_B'W + W'G_B W)/n_B`, `LL_k = -0.5 log MSE_B + mean_B log|f_k'(y)|`. **Null shortcut** (the requested derivation): the sign-flip draw `y*_b = yhat + s_b r` with `s_i = +-1` passes through ANY f exactly as `f(y*_i) = u_i + s_bi d_i` with `u=(f(y)+f(2 yhat - y))/2`, `d=(f(y)-f(2 yhat - y))/2` (two points, two signs). Hence all B=40 draws of all K candidates need ONE extra transform evaluation per row (the reflected point) and one GEMM `D'(d_k * S)` of width K*B=360; `z*'z* = sum u^2 + sum d^2 + 2 sum s u d` and the Jacobian term follow the same identity; the hat-matrix action is replaced by `M c` with the Gram inverse fixed once. Nothing is refitted and no row-level prediction is formed. The design can be capped to the `cap` columns most correlated with the candidates (`cap64` below), so the Gram costs n*cap^2 instead of n*p^2.
**(b) Feature criterion** `tt_b5_cost.feature_criterion` (numba, fused kernel, no p x K x n temporary) and `tt_b5_cost_np.feature_criterion_np` (numpy float32 SIMD ufuncs over 16-column x 2048-row blocks; `cbrt` replaced by `exp(log|x|/3)` because numpy's `cbrt` is a 53 ns scalar libm call): `s_jk = rho^2(f_k(x_j), r + beta_j x_j)` for p columns x K=9 candidates (identity, log|x|, signed sqrt, signed square, cbrt, |x-mu|, ((x-mu)/sd)^2, tanh, exp), the component-plus-residual form so that a raw column's own linear part is not double counted; the permutation null permutes `r` only (R=[r, r_pi1..r_pi40] built once), the transformed block is multiplied by R with one sgemm, and the per-column threshold is the 95 % quantile of `max_{k!=id}(s_k - s_id)` over the 40 draws; accepted if the gain exceeds `max(tau, 0)`. `null_rows` runs the null on a row subsample.

### Correctness against explicit refits (float64, `tt_b5_cost_check.py`, `tt_b5_cost_np_check.py`)
Fast vs reference that REFITS the ridge for every candidate and every draw (n=20000, p=12, K=9, B=12, identical sign matrix): max |dLL| 3.5e-07 (full design) and 2.6e-07 (capped design), max |null max| 4.4e-08 and 1.0e-07, tau equal to 4 digits (0.9007 vs 0.9007). Feature criterion vs an independent numpy float64 implementation with the same permutations: max |s| 1.5e-08, max |null max| 3.9e-10 (K=9 and 10, all rows and subsampled null, with nonzero beta); numpy-SIMD variant vs numba: max |s| 1.2e-08, same best candidate and same accepted columns.

### Does the fast code still decide right (`tt_b5_cost_sanity.py`, 5 seeds, n=20000, B=40)
Target: lognormal -> log accepted 5/5, cube -> cbrt 5/5, hetero lognormal -> log 5/5, linear -> identity (nothing accepted) 5/5 (after the rule `accept only if gain > max(tau, 0)`; without the `max(.,0)` one seed accepted Yeo-Johnson with a NEGATIVE gain). Feature (12 columns, true links log(x3) and 0.7 sqrt(x5), x7 linear): `log_abs` for column 3 and `signed_sqrt` for column 5 accepted in 5/5 seeds, column 7 never; 4 false accepts in 50 noise-or-linear column-seeds (8 %, nominal 5 %, B=40 makes the 95 % quantile coarse, and there is no multiplicity control across columns). Without the partial-residual form the same code chose `1/x` for column 3 (not log): the form matters for correctness, not only for the cost.

### Where the time goes
* Feature criterion: the TRANSFORMS. Per element, one thread, scalar libm in numba (`tt_b5_micro.py`; CPU-time run, the wall run was 1.5-2.5x slower under load): `cbrt` 66 ns, `tanh` 27 ns, `log` 20 ns, `exp` 16 ns, `1/x` 4-7 ns, identity / sqrt / square / abs about 0.6-0.8 ns; the nine candidates cost 133-176 ns per element, of which `cbrt` alone is 38-50 %. The permutation null (B=40) is cheap on top of that because it is one GEMM per block: for p>=200 `null_all` / `obs_only` CPU is only 1.3-1.5x (numba 1.26 / 1.34 / 1.37 at n=1e5 p=200, n=1e5 p=1000, n=1e6 p=200; numpy 1.41 at n=1e6 p=200, 1.47 at n=1e5 p=1000); at p=20 it is 2.3-4x because building the permuted-residual matrix R (40 permutations of n rows) and the sgemm outweigh the 20 columns of transforms. The numpy-SIMD variant is 2.0-2.4x cheaper than the numba kernels in CPU seconds (n=1e5: p=200 1.4 vs 3.4, p=1000 6.8 vs 16.3 for the observed pass) because numpy has AVX2 float32 `log/exp/tanh` loops (3-4.4 ns per element, `micro_np`) and numba here has no SVML (`numba.config.USING_SVML` is False, no `icc_rt`); the remaining cost is the moments and the sgemm (about 30 GFlops, `gemm_probe`). A subsample null (`null_sub50k`) does not pay at n<=1e5 (an extra full observed pass makes it MORE expensive: n=1e5, p=200 2.7 vs 2.2 CPU-s) and saves about 22-27 % at n=1e6 (numpy: 17.1 vs 23.5 CPU-s at p=200; wall 106 vs 144 s at p=1000); its threshold matches the full-null threshold (median column ratio 0.83-1.00, n=1e5, B=100, 3 seeds, `tt_b5_subnull_check.py`).
* Target criterion: for small p, the NULL PASS, not the ridge: n=1e6, p=20 wall 14.5 s null pass vs 1.6 s statistics pass (the work is the two transforms per row, the sign matrix (n x B) and forming the (rows, K, B) right-hand side, about n K B = 3.6e8 element operations, plus the GEMM). For large p with the full design the Gram (n p^2, n=1e6 p=1000: 43.5 s wall) and the null GEMM (33.8 s) are equal order, and both are exactly what ONE ridge fit on all columns costs; the capped design removes the p^2 term (statistics pass 3.4 s) and leaves the screening pass (n p K, 14.7 s) and the null pass (9.0 s). Caveat measured in `tt_b5_subnull_check.py`: the target null is an EFFECT SIZE (tau about 0.7 nats per row at n=1e5, for a lognormal target whose sign-flipped copy is still skewed), NOT a 1/n sampling quantity: a null on 50 000 rows gives the same tau (0.688 / 0.682 / 0.791 vs 0.716 / 0.678 / 0.758 at full n) and must not be rescaled (rescaled it would be 0.34-0.40, half).

### Verdict on cost
* Budget "under 5 % of a fit". At n=20 000, p=20: target criterion 0.09 CPU-s (0.36 s wall) and feature criterion 0.11 CPU-s (0.34 s wall) = 0.04 % + 0.05 % of the default fit in CPU (0.14 % + 0.13 % in contended wall) and 0.5 % + 0.7 % of a `fe_max_steps=0` fit in CPU (1.9 % + 1.8 % in wall). **Acceptable at that size, by a factor of 10 or more.** Larger sizes cannot be judged against an MRMR fit: I ran no MRMR fit beyond n=20 000, p=20, so the ratio there is *not run*. Against a single HGB fit the criteria are not negligible at small sizes (n=2e4, p=20: 0.09-0.11 CPU-s against 1.4 CPU-s, 7 %), and against a single ridge fit they are 20-40x more (0.09 vs 0.00x s), so "cheaper than one fit" is false; they are cheaper than the retraining protocol they replace (K x (B+1) = 369 ridge refits for the target null, K HGB fits for a judge): n=1e5, p=200: 0.7 CPU-s against about 85 CPU-s of 369 ridge fits (0.23 s each, estimate by multiplication, not run) and against 9 HGB fits (about 240 CPU-s, estimate).
* Scaling: both criteria are linear in n. Target: cap64 design costs 0.17-0.27 CPU-s at n=2e4 and 1.2 CPU-s at n=1e5 for any p up to 1000; the full design at p=1000 is as expensive as a ridge fit on all columns (5.7 CPU-s at n=1e5) and should not be used inside MRMR. Feature: 1.9 CPU-s (n=2e4, p=1000, numpy), 10 CPU-s (n=1e5, p=1000), about 90-140 s wall at n=1e6, p=1000: **at p=1000 and n>=1e5 the feature criterion must run on a row subsample** (MRMR's own FE search already works on one; the n=1e5 cells are the cost of a 100 000-row subsample).
* Dominant cost to attack next: `cbrt`/`tanh`/`log`/`exp` scalar evaluations for the feature criterion (SVML or float32 SIMD polynomial kernels would take the 9-candidate pass from about 10-17 ns to a few ns per element-candidate); for the target criterion, the (rows, K, B) right-hand-side construction at small p and the Gram at large p (use the cap).
