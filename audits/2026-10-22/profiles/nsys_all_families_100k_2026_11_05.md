## Correction (same day, `D:/Temp/d2h_attrib.py`)

The `cudaMemcpyAsync` 6.8 s above is mostly the host waiting for the queued kernel behind the copy, not transfer cost: a wrapper on `cp.asnumpy` over one warm fit sums 9.5 s across threads in 5172 reads that move almost nothing (about 330 MB in total, 321 MB of it in ten `quantile_codes_resident_f32` reads at 0.21 s). That matches the ~9 s of kernel time. The largest waits are real kernels: `_honest_null_accept._continuous_accept_block` 2.07 s (one bitset accept kernel), `binned_mi_from_values_gpu` 1178 reads at 1.35 ms (1.59 s, one MI kernel each), `batch_pair_mi_cuda_shared_fused` 0.80 s, `triple_mi_gpu` 0.42 s. So the "batch the readbacks" lever is small; the idle device comes from the many serial one-kernel-then-wait calls (1178 + 843 + 486 + 397 launches of the same few kernels from the main thread), i.e. fusing/batching the CALLS into one launch over several columns, and from the host-only stretches measured in `cpu_gpu_utilization_timeline_2026_11_05.md`.
