
## One NaN operand moved every pair to the double kernel (2026-11-09)

At 30000 x 300 (`F._make`, 308 operand columns, two of them with a NaN) `_float32_unsafe(y, operand_matrix)` was true for the whole batch, so all 49225 pairs ran the float64 warp kernel (1/32 of the single rate on this card): nvprof, two launches 11.0 s (4.3 s + 6.7 s), 27 % of the GPU time of the fit. The same kernel in float32 on a finite 7140-pair block takes 270 ms (about 1.9 s for 49225 pairs; achieved occupancy 0.63, L2 read 112 GB/s, DRAM 13 GB/s, issue slot utilisation 56 %, memory-dependency stalls 59 %, load efficiency 99.99 %). Fix: `batch_pair_usability_corr_cuda_warp` now decides per operand (`_float32_unsafe_columns`): the pairs that touch a non-finite or offset-heavy operand run in double, every other pair in float32; a non-finite or offset-heavy target still sends everything to double. Test: `test_one_nan_operand_moves_only_its_own_pairs_to_the_double_kernel` (values equal the double reference to 1e-6 for the double pairs and 5e-4 for the float32 pairs).

Measured end to end, `score_prospective_pairs` at 30000 x 300 (same 313 operands, 49225 candidate pairs, the same 35 surviving pairs): 13.7 s before, 7.4 s after.

bench-attempt-rejected: a one-warp-per-pair kernel that scores all nine forms from one pass over the rows (to cut the traffic of a pair to that of one form). First cut, 7140 pairs x 9 forms x 30000 rows in the double path: 2.33 s against 1.12-1.25 s for the one-warp-per-(pair, form) kernel, and its values did not match the reference (max difference 2.6e-2 on 50k of 64k pairs; a single-form launch was also wrong, so a logic defect in the first cut, not the idea). The nvprof counters above also say the existing kernel is not DRAM bound (13 GB/s of 112), so the traffic reduction would not have paid. Reverted, not shipped.

Remaining `score_prospective_pairs` at 30000 x 300 after the fix (cProfile of the one call, 7.0 s): numba CUDA synchronisation 2.8 s (181 calls: the float32 usability kernel, the double part for the 2 NaN operands, and the pair gates' device work), `_conditional_maxt._conditional_mi_columns` 1.25 s (205 calls; 40 permutations x 313 candidates x 30000 rows of counts on the host, parallel njit: a device twin of this count table would need a (strata, levels, classes) table per candidate in shared memory), `record_fe_rejection` 0.55 s for 48793 records (Python per pair), `_prepass_gate_and_usability_candidates` 0.2 s own time. Each is under 20 % of the call; none is worth a restructure on its own.

## Launches per call and whole-GPU throughput (read from the measurements above, 2026-11-13)

Launches per call: `batch_pair_usability_corr_cuda_warp` splits the pairs by operand safety and launches the warp kernel once for the float32 pairs and once for the double pairs, so a call is at most two kernel launches whatever the number of pairs (the 30000 x 300 fit measured exactly two, 4.3 s and 6.7 s before the per-operand split); the pair index tables are uploaded once per call. Whole-GPU throughput of the float32 launch (7140-pair block, 270 ms, nvprof): achieved occupancy 0.63, L2 read throughput 112 GB/s, DRAM throughput 13 GB/s, issue-slot utilization 56 %, load efficiency 99.99 %. The kernel is bound by memory-dependency stalls (59 %) on the L2, not by DRAM, so the device is busy but not bandwidth-saturated; the double launch runs at 1/32 of the single rate on this card, which is why only the operands that need it go there. The backend choice (CPU against the warp kernel) now goes through the kernel-tuning cache entry `fe_pair_usability_corr_backend`; its sweep at 500, 5000 and 50000 pairs (5000 rows, 60 operands, float32) picks the device in every cell, 0.047 s against 0.347 s at 5000 pairs and 0.74 s against 3.48 s at 50000.
